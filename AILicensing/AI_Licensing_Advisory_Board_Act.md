# A BILL

To establish the Artificial Intelligence Licensing Advisory Board to study and make recommendations regarding a system of licensing for the development, production, and use of artificial intelligence, and for other purposes.

</div>

---

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

## SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

This Act may be cited as the "**Artificial Intelligence Licensing Advisory Board Act of 2027**".

## SEC. 2. FINDINGS.

Congress finds the following:

1. Artificial intelligence systems are being developed, produced, and deployed at a rapid pace and are increasingly used in matters affecting public safety, national security, the economy, civil rights, health, and the integrity of information.
2. Federal and State law has long required licenses, certifications, or registrations for the production and use of technologies, materials, and professional services that carry significant risk to the public, including in the fields of nuclear materials, aviation, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, telecommunications, and the practice of medicine, law, and engineering.
3. There is currently no comprehensive Federal framework for determining whether, or under what conditions, the development, production, or use of artificial intelligence should be subject to licensing, nor for ensuring compliance with and monitoring of any such licensing.
4. Before Congress considers whether to establish such a framework, it would benefit from the independent, expert advice of individuals from academia and industry with deep knowledge of artificial intelligence and its technical, economic, legal, and societal implications.

## SEC. 3. ESTABLISHMENT.

There is established in the legislative branch an independent advisory board to be known as the "**Artificial Intelligence Licensing Advisory Board**" (in this Act referred to as the "Board").

## SEC. 4. DUTIES.

**(a) IN GENERAL.**—The Board shall conduct a comprehensive study of, and develop recommendations regarding, whether and how the Federal Government should establish a system of licensing for the development, production, and use of artificial intelligence.

**(b) MATTERS TO BE CONSIDERED.**—In carrying out subsection (a), the Board shall consider—

1. whether a system of licensing for the development or production of artificial intelligence is advisable and, if so, the potential scope, structure, and administration of such a system;
2. whether a system of licensing for the use or deployment of artificial intelligence is advisable and, if so, the potential scope, structure, and administration of such a system;
3. methods and mechanisms for ensuring compliance with, and for monitoring and auditing, any such system of licensing;
4. methods for the enforcement of any such system of licensing, including whether civil penalties, administrative sanctions, or criminal penalties, including criminal indictment and prosecution, would be appropriate for violations, and the circumstances under which each would be appropriate;
5. which Federal department or agency, existing or new, would be best suited to administer any such system of licensing, and the respective roles of Federal, State, local, and Tribal governments;
6. existing licensing, certification, and registration regimes in other fields, and any lessons such regimes offer for the licensing of artificial intelligence;
7. approaches to the licensing or regulation of artificial intelligence that have been adopted or proposed by other countries and by international bodies, and opportunities for international coordination;
8. the potential effects of any such system of licensing on innovation, competition, small businesses, academic and nonprofit research, open-source development, national security, and the economic competitiveness of the United States; and
9. such other matters as the Board determines relevant.

**(c) NO AUTHORITY TO REGULATE.**—Nothing in this Act shall be construed to grant the Board authority to issue, deny, or revoke any license, to promulgate any regulation, or to impose any requirement on any person. The role of the Board is solely advisory.

## SEC. 5. MEMBERSHIP.

**(a) NUMBER AND APPOINTMENT.**—The Board shall be composed of 15 members, of whom—

1. 3 members shall be appointed by the Speaker of the House of Representatives;
2. 3 members shall be appointed by the minority leader of the House of Representatives;
3. 3 members shall be appointed by the majority leader of the Senate;
4. 3 members shall be appointed by the minority leader of the Senate; and
5. 3 members shall be appointed by the President.

**(b) QUALIFICATIONS.**—

1. **IN GENERAL.**—Each member of the Board shall be an individual who has recognized expertise in artificial intelligence, computer science, or a field directly relevant to the duties of the Board, and who is—
   - (A) an academic expert, meaning an individual who holds, or has held, a faculty or research appointment at an institution of higher education or a nonprofit research institution; or
   - (B) an industry expert, meaning an individual with substantial professional experience in the research, development, production, or deployment of artificial intelligence in the private sector.
2. **BALANCE.**—Of the 3 members appointed by each appointing official under subsection (a), at least 1 shall be an academic expert and at least 1 shall be an industry expert. The appointing officials shall consult with one another with the goal of ensuring that the Board as a whole reflects a balance of academic and industry expertise and a diversity of technical and disciplinary perspectives, including expertise in law, ethics, economics, and security as they relate to artificial intelligence.
3. **FEDERAL OFFICERS AND EMPLOYEES.**—An individual appointed to the Board may not be an officer or employee of the Federal Government, other than an individual serving in a part-time or advisory capacity.

**(c) CONFLICTS OF INTEREST.**—Each member of the Board shall, before taking office and annually thereafter, publicly disclose any financial interest, employment, or other affiliation that is relevant to the duties of the Board. The Board shall adopt written procedures for the recusal of members with respect to matters in which a member has a conflict of interest.

**(d) DEADLINE FOR APPOINTMENT.**—Each member of the Board shall be appointed not later than 90 days after the date of enactment of this Act.

**(e) TERM.**—Each member shall be appointed for the life of the Board.

**(f) VACANCIES.**—A vacancy in the Board shall not affect its powers and shall be filled in the same manner as the original appointment.

**(g) CHAIRPERSON AND VICE CHAIRPERSON.**—The Board shall select a Chairperson and a Vice Chairperson from among its members, one of whom shall be an academic expert and one of whom shall be an industry expert.

**(h) MEETINGS.**—

1. **INITIAL MEETING.**—The Board shall hold its first meeting not later than 30 days after the date on which a majority of the members have been appointed.
2. **SUBSEQUENT MEETINGS.**—The Board shall meet at the call of the Chairperson or a majority of its members.
3. **QUORUM.**—A majority of the members of the Board shall constitute a quorum, but a lesser number may hold hearings.

**(i) COMPENSATION AND TRAVEL EXPENSES.**—

1. **COMPENSATION.**—Each member of the Board may be compensated at a rate not to exceed the daily equivalent of the annual rate of basic pay prescribed for level IV of the Executive Schedule under section 5315 of title 5, United States Code, for each day (including travel time) during which the member is engaged in the actual performance of the duties of the Board.
2. **TRAVEL EXPENSES.**—Each member of the Board shall be allowed travel expenses, including per diem in lieu of subsistence, in accordance with sections 5702 and 5703 of title 5, United States Code.

## SEC. 6. POWERS OF THE BOARD.

**(a) HEARINGS AND EVIDENCE.**—The Board may, for the purpose of carrying out this Act, hold such hearings, sit and act at such times and places, take such testimony, and receive such evidence as the Board considers appropriate.

**(b) INFORMATION FROM FEDERAL AGENCIES.**—The Board may secure directly from any Federal department or agency such information as the Board considers necessary to carry out this Act. Upon request of the Chairperson, the head of such department or agency shall furnish such information to the Board, subject to applicable law regarding the protection of classified and proprietary information.

**(c) CONSULTATION.**—In carrying out its duties, the Board shall consult with, and solicit public comment from, a broad range of stakeholders, including the National Institute of Standards and Technology, other relevant Federal agencies, State governments, civil society organizations, labor organizations, consumer advocates, and members of the public.

**(d) POSTAL SERVICES.**—The Board may use the United States mails in the same manner and under the same conditions as other departments and agencies of the Federal Government.

**(e) GIFTS.**—The Board may accept, use, and dispose of gifts or donations of services or property, except that the Board may not accept any gift or donation from any person or entity that develops, produces, or deploys artificial intelligence for commercial purposes.

**(f) CONTRACTS.**—The Board may enter into contracts with, and make other arrangements with, public agencies, private organizations, and individuals to the extent necessary to carry out its duties.

## SEC. 7. STAFF.

**(a) EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR.**—The Chairperson, in consultation with the Vice Chairperson, may appoint and fix the compensation of an executive director and such other personnel as may be necessary to enable the Board to carry out its duties, without regard to the provisions of title 5, United States Code, governing appointments in the competitive service, and without regard to the provisions of chapter 51 and subchapter III of chapter 53 of such title relating to classification and General Schedule pay rates, except that the rate of pay may not exceed the rate payable for level V of the Executive Schedule under section 5316 of such title.

**(b) DETAILEES.**—Upon request of the Chairperson, the head of any Federal department or agency may detail, on a reimbursable or nonreimbursable basis, any of the personnel of that department or agency to the Board to assist it in carrying out its duties, without interruption or loss of civil service status or privilege.

**(c) EXPERTS AND CONSULTANTS.**—The Chairperson may procure temporary and intermittent services under section 3109(b) of title 5, United States Code.

**(d) SECURITY CLEARANCES.**—The appropriate Federal departments and agencies shall cooperate with the Board in expeditiously providing to members and staff of the Board appropriate security clearances, to the extent practicable, pursuant to existing procedures and requirements.

## SEC. 8. REPORTS.

**(a) INTERIM REPORT.**—Not later than 1 year after the date of the initial meeting of the Board, the Board shall submit to the President and to the Congress an interim report on the progress of the Board.

**(b) FINAL REPORT.**—Not later than 2 years after the date of the initial meeting of the Board, the Board shall submit to the President and to the Congress a final report containing—

1. a detailed statement of the findings and conclusions of the Board with respect to each of the matters described in section 4(b);
2. the recommendations of the Board, including any recommendations for legislative or administrative action; and
3. any dissenting or additional views of individual members of the Board.

**(c) PUBLIC AVAILABILITY.**—Each report submitted under this section shall be made publicly available on a website of the Board, except that any classified information may be included in a classified annex.

## SEC. 9. TERMINATION.

The Board shall terminate 90 days after the date on which it submits its final report under section 8(b).

## SEC. 10. APPLICABILITY OF FEDERAL ADVISORY COMMITTEE PROVISIONS.

Chapter 10 of title 5, United States Code (commonly referred to as the "Federal Advisory Committee Act"), shall not apply to the Board, except that the Board shall, to the extent practicable and consistent with the protection of classified and proprietary information, hold its meetings and hearings in public and make its records available to the public.

## SEC. 11. DEFINITIONS.

In this Act:

1. **ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.**—The term "artificial intelligence" has the meaning given such term in section 5002 of the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9401).
2. **INSTITUTION OF HIGHER EDUCATION.**—The term "institution of higher education" has the meaning given such term in section 101 of the Higher Education Act of 1965 (20 U.S.C. 1001).

## SEC. 12. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS.

There are authorized to be appropriated to carry out this Act such sums as may be necessary for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2029, to remain available until expended.

### Drafter's Note (not part of bill text)

This draft is modeled on the structure of prior congressionally-established study commissions and advisory boards, including:

- the **National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States** (Pub. L. 107–306, title VI) — membership appointed by congressional leadership, powers, staff, and reporting provisions;
- the **Commission on Wartime Contracting in Iraq and Afghanistan** (Pub. L. 110–181, sec. 841) — interim/final report structure and termination;
- the **National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence** (Pub. L. 115–232, sec. 1051) — subject-matter scope and qualifications of members; and
- the **Commission on Evidence-Based Policymaking Act of 2016** (Pub. L. 114–140) — balance of academic and practitioner/industry expertise.

Section numbers, committee referrals, appointment allocations, compensation levels, and deadlines are placeholders for the sponsor's consideration and should be reviewed by the House Office of the Legislative Counsel.
